Analysing the Desirability of Low-Tech Solutions

Client Project

Buildings account for 44% of France’s energy use (2023). The August 2023 heatwave and the April 2025 Iberian blackout showed that buildings must rely less on energy and keep people comfortable when systems fail. Low-tech solutions do this with less, yet few in the industry ask for them.

Approach
Stakeholder mapping across five groups (project owners, consultants, designers, main contractors and end users), expert interviews and a questionnaire, then scoring five solutions against each group’s top priorities.
Key Findings
Natural ventilation, free cooling and mechanical switches (over sensors) performed best, yet stakeholders preferred bio-sourced insulation and reused materials. Cost came first for almost every group, and behaviour change, not technology, was the biggest barrier.
Recommendations
Communicate the benefits of the best performers, and lower cost and compliance hurdles for the solutions people already prefer. Each stakeholder group gets its own engagement plan, such as case studies and site visits for contractors, and workshops for end users.

Key Takeaway

Behaviour change, not technology, is the real barrier. The solutions already work; people need reasons to choose them.

Also Delivered

A stakeholder database and method the client can reuse to test more low-tech solutions.

Autonomous Vehicles: Who Captures the Value?

Strategy Research

In February 2026, Uber launched Uber Autonomous Solutions, a platform covering everything above the vehicle: demand, data, fleet operations and insurance.

Approach
Hypothesis-driven, with a MECE issue tree across three questions: is the market accessible, which strategy gives the best risk-adjusted return, and what capabilities must be in place. Backed by interviews with experts at Aptiv and Kia Connect, and research from Goldman Sachs, McKinsey and Deloitte.
Key Findings
The bottleneck has moved from technology to commercialisation. Value is shifting to whoever owns the customer and the data. In Europe, the accessible market sits in a handful of dense city corridors, and the window to act is about 18 to 24 months.
Recommendations
Three moves, in order. Supply vehicles to Uber now, on non-exclusive terms with data rights, to fund the next step. Within six months, launch the carmaker’s own mobility service in one EU city, licensing the self-driving technology instead of building it. Keep full vertical integration as a five-year option through a minority stake in a technology partner.
